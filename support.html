How to play

  • Campaign starts you in Downing Street as the party in government, or calls a snap election as any party you choose.
  • Each turn is a month. Plan it in four tabs: the Red box (the month's decisions and your three missions), Budget (taxes and spending, at the Autumn Budget in November and the Spring Statement in March), Parliament (policies, new bills and your Cabinet, with the month's plan at the top) and Country (where everything is heading and why, the voters and how the party, the papers and the world see you). The Treasury, from the figures at the top of Budget, shows the money five years ahead.
  • Press the month button at the foot of the sheet, and the month's business follows in order: your decisions, Prime Minister's Questions, the Budget when it's due, each bill as a division in the Commons, and then the report on what changed and why.
  • Policies, bills, whipping the House and reshuffles cost political capital, shown as a bolt and a number beside each. The Budget costs none: its risks are the vote on the Finance Bill, the markets and the voters. Good months win capital back (Prime Minister's Questions, by-elections, bills passed) and defeats, rebellions and U-turns lose it. Tap the capital at the top to see what you're spending and when you'll have enough.
  • Before a vote the whips tell you how it looks; in the division each MP makes up their own mind. MPs in marginal seats rebel when a bill hurts them, groups of MPs vote together against what crosses them, and if the backbenchers turn on you they force a confidence vote.
  • Your three missions are three of your manifesto's pledges. You can change them in a term's first three months, and they're judged when the election is called: each one met helps your campaign, each one missed hurts it.
  • Call the election from the seal button at the top, or wait until it's due. On election night, watch your own seat and the seat that decides your majority.
  • Lose, and you lead the opposition: ask the questions at PMQs, choose which government bills to oppose, spend your days in the news on the government's openings, Opposition Days and by-elections, set your positions and launch flagship policies, and keep your MPs' morale up until the next election. If morale stays too low, your MPs choose a new leader and you carry on as them.
  • Simulation starts from the latest polling average. Move the sliders to see the House those shares would elect.

Questions

Where do the polls and seats come from? The game checks a public data file when it opens. It's rebuilt twice a day from the polling average on Wikipedia and the UK Parliament Members API. Without a connection, the game uses the last copy it saw.

Are real politicians in it? No. Parties are real and their positions are simplified. Every person in the game is invented, and you name your own Prime Minister.

Is my game saved? Yes, on your iPhone or iPad, after every turn. Continue picks it up from the main menu. Delete it in Settings.

It's too easy, or too hard. Change the difficulty in Settings. Easy shows the exact seats each decision is worth; Hard makes the markets and your backbenchers less forgiving.

Is it on iPad? Yes. On iPad the controls sit in a panel beside the map, and the Budget, divisions and the monthly report use the whole screen. Your iPhone and iPad keep separate saved games.
